What it actually does

Real walking routes

The street grid is baked in, so distances follow actual pavement — not straight lines through people's back gardens.

Set times respected

It solves for what you can physically reach: every stop fits inside that band's window with time to walk there — and hits your band count if the clock allows.

Pin your must-sees

Star the friend's band you're obliged to watch and the route is built around it, filling the gaps with things you'll like.

Share as a link

The whole plan packs into the URL. Send it and your friends open the same route and map — no app, no account.

Straight to your calendar

Export the schedule as .ics and every stop lands in your phone with the address and a one-line description.

Works with no signal

Map, data and routing are all in the page. Once it loads it needs nothing — handy in a crowd on a saturated cell tower.

How it works

Set your afternoon

When you're free, where you're starting and finishing, how long you like to linger at a porch, and how fast you walk.

Turn the dials

Ten sliders, each centred on "don't care". Nudge one and the route redraws as you go — turn country down and those sets fall out of the schedule and off the map immediately. Seek or avoid whole genres, and star any must-sees.

Say how many bands

Ask for exactly six, or between three and five, or just "as many as fit". It searches thousands of possible afternoons and keeps the best one that obeys you — an ordered schedule, walking legs between stops, and a drawn map.

Take it with you

Share the link, export the calendar, or open the whole thing as walking directions.

Doors are on the porch

Free, no sign-in, nothing stored anywhere but your own browser.
