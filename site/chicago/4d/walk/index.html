The chip grades the POSITION, not the building: a tavern can be documented down to its landlord and its sign and still stand where no source puts it, because what survives is usually a street and a side of it rather than a corner. The grade is the record's own — the same value the building card shows when you arrive — so the two cannot drift apart. Viewpoints are authored camera positions, not claims about the town, and junctions are the survey control this project georeferenced from.

The default, 1.68 m, is the mean stature of an adult man in the 1830s United States — this walkthrough puts your eye where a person of the period had theirs, which is lower than a modern viewer expects and lower than the prairie grass beside you, because both of those are true of the place. Raise it if the view is uncomfortable; it changes only the camera, never the town.

Navigation help
  • attested — a source attests this at the scene date
  • inferred — reasoned from evidence about this thing: a described location, a measured lot, an adjacent record
  • reconstructed — no source speaks to this one; built to fill a demonstrable need of the town, and drawn as dithered massing

Every building is clickable. The tint and the citation come from the same record, so they cannot drift apart. Dimensional uncertainty is separate: a building can be well documented in character and still have a guessed size, and the popup says so.

Straight from the project's append-only docs/LIBERTIES.md. Every value a record states without evidence is named in this list — an invented outline or placement, and equally a roof, a height or a porch chosen because it was usual rather than because it was found. An entry says which inventions it admits to — the dashed chips — and the commit gate checks those claims against the records both ways, failing if an invention has no admission or an admission has no invention. The rule now runs the other way as well: where a record states something the model does not build — a stable, a painted sign, a chimney count no generator reads — the popup marks that row not built or not modelled from this, and it too has to be named here. A well-evidenced feature you cannot see is as much a gap as an invention you can.

The ground you are standing on

The buildings are not the only things making claims here. The terrain is a research record too — a documented water surface, three division levels argued out of period narrative feet, a bank face and a river cross-section that no source describes at all — and it dithers under the confidence view like everything else while never saying what was graded. These are its own statements, each with its figures, its reasoning and its citation, straight from the epoch's terrain_spec.json. Where a claim has no reasoning recorded, it says so.
