Render kit — turning these plans into accurate images

The side elevations are demoted on purpose: they're rough massing sketches. The photoreal renders are what sell a design — so the workflow is to hand a stronger image model (GPT / DALL·E) the measured top-down plan and the exact shopping list for a design, generate a render, then drop it into that design's render slot. This kit is the prompt and the rules that make the result match the plan.

What went wrong in the first pass. The earlier renders were close, but the borders came out far too dense — plants packed shoulder-to-shoulder into a solid wall, with no soil, no mulch, and far more plants than the plan calls for. Hostas in a new planting sit apart, each as a distinct clump with dark mulch between them; they only touch years later at mature spread. The prompt below fixes that explicitly.

Master prompt (paste, then append the design's data)

Photorealistic photograph of a narrow shade garden border planted along a Chicago
"gangway" — the 10-foot-wide grass-and-path corridor between two tall brick bungalows.
Overcast, soft even daylight. Camera at standing eye height (~5.5 ft), looking down the
length of the corridor so the bed recedes toward a vanishing point.

THE SITE (fixed for every render):
- Two 22-ft red-brick bungalow walls, one on each side, in deep shade. A simple path
  or lawn strip runs down the middle.
- The planting bed is ONE continuous strip, only 3 FEET DEEP, running ~95 feet along
  ONE wall. It is NOT a wide mass planting.
- Dark hardwood-mulch soil is clearly visible BETWEEN plants.

PLANTING RULES (this is where past renders failed — follow exactly):
- Plants are spaced at their mature spread and are NOT yet touching: each hosta is a
  SEPARATE, distinct clump with a visible ring of dark mulch around it. Sparse, freshly
  planted, breathing room — NOT a dense continuous hedge or wall of foliage.
- Match the COUNTS and VARIETIES in the list below. Do not invent extra plants or fill
  every gap. If in doubt, use FEWER plants, more mulch.
- The front edge is a single thin ribbon of small mouse-ear hostas — one plant deep,
  low and neat, with mulch showing between them. Not a solid border.
- Keep leaf colors true to the varieties named (blue = dusty blue-grey; gold = soft
  chartreuse; white-margined = green leaf with a clean creamy-white edge).
- Realistic nursery-size plants (1-gallon), not giant mature specimens.

STYLE: natural documentary garden photography, shallow-to-medium depth of field, no
people, no text, no labels, true-to-life proportions for a 3-ft-deep bed.

— THEN PASTE, from the design you're rendering —
DESIGN NAME + the SHOPPING LIST table (variety, mature spread, quantity), read
street-end (left, a little more light) to the deep shade slot. Tell it the dominant
color story in one line (e.g. "blue anchors, crisp white margins, blue mouse-ear edge").

How to use it

1. Pick a design (or a variation) above. Each has a top-down plan and a shopping list — those are the source of truth.

2. Copy the master prompt, then paste that design's shopping list and its one-line color story underneath.

3. Generate. Ask for a couple of variations. When one matches the plan, save it and drop it into that design's render slot.

If the borders still look too dense, add: "Reduce the number of plants by a third, double the visible mulch between clumps, and make sure no two plants touch."

Feeding it the data

The base plant data (spread, colour, light, price, stock) lives in the Plant finder — you can export it as JSON or hand a fresh re-pull to a model with the Lurvey data spec. Everything the renderer needs is in each design's shopping list.
