The post-office list of letters remaining at Chicago on 1 October 1834 is the census proxy
ruling 1 asks for, and October 1834 (T-0292) read it at name level: 179 names, from the
1834-10-22 printing, page 1 columns 1 and 2. THE FIRST OF ITS FOUR NAME SUB-COLUMNS — the
alphabet from A to about H, roughly 130 printed rows — IS NOT AMONG THEM, and it is not
among them in any of the three printings either.

## What is actually wrong

The list ran three times: 1834-10-08 page 3 columns 1-2, 1834-10-15 page 3 columns 3-4,
1834-10-22 page 1 columns 1-2. In every one, the segmenter's crop lands so that the
left-most sub-column is cut down its LEFT edge and survives as name endings only:

    1834-10-22 p1 c1   `Jadeewe, David` `fae Wiliam` `at eilney` `Ateater, Jose`
    1834-10-15 p3 c3   `we, David` `ze, Williem` `t, Jesse` `ums, William-`
    1834-10-08 p3 c1   `han 2` `Poe` `id` `ardy`

Six rows are legible whole in the 1834-10-22 crop and were minted (Benson, John H.; Bowen,
Erastus; Bowen, Albert W.; Brown, Jas.; Hancock, Harry [B.]; Hopkins, Hannah). The rest
are recorded as absent.

## Why it is worth a ticket rather than a shrug

THE MISSING HALF IS RECOVERABLE, and one issue already carries both halves. In 1834-10-15
the name BEGINNINGS survive at the RIGHT edge of page 3 column 2 (`Andrey`, `Abell`,
`Atwate`, `Akers`, `Beilly`, `Bryant`, `Barlow`, `Brooks`, `Bird`, `Chipms`…) and the
ENDINGS at the LEFT edge of column 3 (`we, David`, `ze, Williem`…), row for row. Joining
them by sense reconstructs the sub-column — `Andrey` + `we, David` is `Andrews, David` —
but a claim's quote comes from ONE column, so the join cannot live inside one locator and
T-0292 did not attempt it.

## What a run should do

Either (a) make it two claims, one per column, with the join written out in the notes and
the entities minted from the column that carries the surnames, saying plainly that the
reading is a two-column join; or (b) read the sub-column off the page images and mint it
`scan_verified`, which outranks the transcription and settles it. (b) is the better answer
and needs the deposit's PDFs, not just its text.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — one demonstration, never weakened to pass)

- The A-to-H sub-column of the 1 October 1834 list is extracted, and the PR states how many
  names it holds against the ~130 printed rows.
- The join method is written down in the claims themselves, not only in the PR.
- No name is minted twice against the 179 already in the gazetteer from 1834-10-22.
- The gate is green and the gazetteer recompiles.
