D. Weaver's standing notice of a building for sale or to let on Lot 2, block 1, North Water
street runs in five consecutive numbers of the *Chicago Democrat*, and the five do not agree
on the day its own copy is dated:

| issue | claim | the dateline, verbatim | reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1834-11-26 | c010 | `ine. 12, 1881-50` (line 2508, not claimed) | 12? |
| 1834-12-03 | c025 | `Chicago. Nor. 12, 1831. SO WEAVER.` | **12** |
| 1834-12-10 | c012 | `e, 1631.—00` | unread |
| 1834-12-17 | c006 | `3, Nor. 13, 131-D WEAVER.` | 13 |
| 1834-12-24 | c012 | `Chicago, Nor. 19, 1834-SD. WEAVER.` | **19** |

T-0328 settled the LOT number across these same five printings — four set Lot 2, one sets
Lot 9 — and deliberately did not touch the dateline, because that is a separate question and
a ticket is not widened mid-run. But it left the corpus in a state worth naming: the two
claims that predate T-0328 (1834-12-17 c006 and 1834-12-24 c012) BOTH carry
`ad_copy_date.iso: 1834-11-19`, asserted when 1834-12-24 was the only legible dateline in
evidence. Two printings now read 12, and 1834-12-17 — the column T-0328 showed to be the
worst of the five on digits — reads 13. The three claims T-0328 added assert no
`ad_copy_date` at all rather than pick a side.

So an assertion stands in the data that a majority of the witnesses may contradict, and
nothing says so except the claim notes.

Note the shape of the evidence is NOT the same as T-0328's. There the disputed digit sat in
a clean line in four of five printings; here three of the five datelines are themselves
wreckage (`ine. 12, 1881-50`, `e, 1631.—00`, `3, Nor. 13, 131`), so a majority count is a
weaker instrument. `12` and `19` differ by a whole character rather than a glyph, which
argues one of the settings is a compositor's error rather than a transcription's — and the
notice first appears on 1834-11-26, so both candidate days precede its first printing and
neither is excluded on that ground.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- Every one of the five datelines is transcribed and set out, including the two that are
  currently unclaimed or unread.
- Either the day is settled with the reasoning stated, or it is recorded as unsettled — and
  in EITHER case the two claims asserting `1834-11-19` are brought into line with the answer.
  An assertion that outruns its evidence is the defect here; leaving it in place unremarked
  is not an outcome.
- No transcription is edited to reach agreement (T-0294's rule, and T-0328 kept it).
- `docs/RESEARCH/weaver_building_north_water_block_1.md` § *What is still open* is updated
  or removed to match.
