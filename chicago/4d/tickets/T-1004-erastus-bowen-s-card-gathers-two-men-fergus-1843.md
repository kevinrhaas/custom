Erastus Bowen's card gathers two men: Fergus 1843 prints the city collector and an Erastus Selden Bowen who was sixteen in 1835.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

**FOUND ON THE WAY PAST T-0990 COHORT C1**, ruling the tract purchaser BOWEN ERASTUS.

The card `bowen_erastus_selden` is graded G2b out of the tax list of 1833, the poll of
1834, the Calumet Club's roll of early Chicago and directory lines of 1843 and 1844. Its
NAME, though, comes from a Fergus 1843 line that belongs to a different man. Fergus 1843
prints two:

| printed | what it says |
|---|---|
| `Bowen, Erastus (B. & Cole), res 40 Michigan ave [died. Aurora, Kane Co., Ill.]` | the man Fergus 1839 prints as `Bowen, Erastus, city collector, So. Water st, cor. Michigan ave.` and Norris 1844 as `Bowen, Erastus, of B. & Cole, house Michigan avenue` |
| `Bowen, Erastus Selden [veterinary surgeon] [died Oct. 19, 1888, a. 69]` | dead in 1888 aged 69, so born about 1819 — **sixteen years old on the scene date**, and not the man on an 1833 tax list |

The card carries the elder man's documents under the younger man's name, and every
downstream reading of it — the tract entries T-0990 upheld among them — is about the
elder. Two Michigan-avenue addresses landing on one card is how the fold got away with it.

**What a run would do.** Settle which documents belong to which man, keep the elder on
the 1835 card under the name his own volumes print (`Erastus Bowen`, city collector), and
decide whether the younger has any 1835 record at all — if he has none, he does not belong
in the layer and the `Selden` should come off the id rather than off the person. The
T-0990 ruling stands either way: it was ruled against the elder's documents and says so.
