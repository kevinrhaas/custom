Is the Democrat's 'A. Sweet' of 4 June 1834 Alanson Sweet, the Alonson Sweet of 16 July
1834, or a third man.

**Raised by T-1155**, which repaired the name splitter and in doing so created the
question. The Chicago Democrat prints three Sweets in the scene window:

| reading | locator | identity today |
|---|---|---|
| `Alanson Sweet` | `chicago_democrat_1834_12_24#c011`, and the 1833 tax list, the 1834 poll and the 1835 poll | `id_sweet_alanson` |
| `Alon[s]on Sweet` | `chicago_democrat_1834_07_16#c019` | `id_sweet_alonson` |
| `A. Sweet` | `chicago_democrat_1834_06_04#c023` | `id_sweet_a` — its own card, `hh_sweet_a` |

Until T-1155 the middle reading lost its supplied `s` to the splitter and came out as
forenames `alon` and `on`, which is no full forename, so the initial-only `A. Sweet` met
exactly ONE candidate in the surname and was folded onto Alanson. With the supply kept
`Alonson` is a full forename carrying the same initial, and the consolidation's rule —
an initial attaches to a full forename only when one carries it, two rivals being a
refusal — correctly declines to choose. The fold was made by a damaged parse; the
refusal is honest. But a refusal is not an answer, and the town now carries a container
(L220's 392nd) written round an initial.

Three readings are possible and the sources may settle them: `Alanson` and `Alonson` may
be one man the compositor set two ways — the pattern C7/C9 already record for Van Den
Bogart, Kimberly/Kimberley and Clybourn/Clybourne — in which case `A. Sweet` folds onto
the survivor and `hh_sweet_a` is withdrawn; or they are two men, in which case the 4 June
printing cannot be assigned and the container stands as it is; or the 4 June printing is
a third Sweet. Nothing here may be settled on resemblance: the rule this project uses
wants a page setting the two spellings for the same person, not a judgement that they
look alike.

**Acceptance:** read `chicago_democrat_1834_06_04#c023`, `#c019` of 16 July 1834 and
`#c011` of 24 December 1834 as printed, in full context, and say what each names and what
it is about — a notice, a list, an advertisement — because the context is what can tie an
initial to a man. Rule the Alanson/Alonson pair under an existing C-rule or refuse it with
the reason written on BOTH cards. Then rule `A. Sweet`: fold it under a stated rule, or
refuse it and say on `hh_sweet_a` that the refusal is the finding. Whatever the outcome,
L220's count and its prose move with it, `card_merge_rulings.json` carries the ruling if
anything folds, and no confidence anywhere is raised to reach it.
