'Julia K. Clark, half-brother of A. Clybourne, then living with him' — a garbled forename, a person written into a household that does not hold one, and two Archibald Clybourn(e) cards.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

**Found by T-0734**, the pass that wrote the kinship the sources already state.

`hh_clybourne_archibald/persons[0]/note` carries this, from Andreas's roster:

> 'Julia K. Clark, half-brother of A. Clybourne, then living with him' is named in the
> next clause and is written into this household.

Three separate faults stand in one sentence, and not one of them is a kinship:

1. **The forename is garbled as read.** "Julia K. Clark" is given as a half-BROTHER. The
   town holds `clark_john_k` — John K. Clark — as an attested head of his own household,
   which is what the initial and the surname point at, but the reading has never been
   made against the printed page.
2. **The sentence says he "is written into this household" and he is not.** The record
   holds exactly one person, the head. Either the writing was lost or the sentence was
   aspirational; either way the record states something about itself that is false.
3. **There are two Archibald Clybourn(e) cards** — `clybourn_archibald` and
   `clybourne_archibald` — so even a correct reading has two records to choose between,
   and that duplicate is under separate ruling.

A half brother is precisely the degree the kin vocabulary exists to keep straight
(T-0597), and it must not be landed on a reading nobody has made.

**Acceptance:** the printed clause is read against the page and the forename settled or
declared unreadable; the record's claim that the man "is written into this household" is
made true or withdrawn; and IF the identity with `clark_john_k` is ruled — on the
crosswalk's rules, against a settled Clybourne card — the reciprocal `half_brother` rows
are written onto both records and `tools/harvest_stated_kinship.py` carries the ruling.
No half brother is landed on a guess at an initial.
