Andreas prints 'Julia K. Clark, half-brother of A. Clybourne, then living with him' and the town holds a John K Clark: the forename is damaged and nobody has ruled on it.

Found by T-0734, the pass that wrote the kinship the sources already state. Andreas's 1833
roster, quoted verbatim on `hh_clybourne_archibald`, reads:

> 'Julia K. Clark, half-brother of A. Clybourne, then living with him'

Two things are wrong with the state that leaves behind:

1. **The forename is damaged and nobody has ruled on it.** The entry says *half-brother* and
   prints a female forename. `hh_clark_john_k` — 'John K Clark', a civic-mint card off the
   1833/1834 poll and tax lists — is the obvious candidate and T-0734 refused to bind it,
   because that is an OCR correction plus an identity ruling and not a kinship pass's call.
   Rule it: either the two are one man, in which case a reciprocal `half_brother` kin row goes
   on both records (the relation is in the vocabulary as of T-0734), or they are not, and the
   refusal is written where the next run can read it.
2. **The Clybourne card says Clark 'is named in the next clause and is written into this
   household' — and he is not.** `hh_clybourne_archibald` carries exactly one person. Either
   write him or stop saying he is written.

The roster also says 'then living with him', which if the identity holds is a residence claim
about a man whose own card says 'no dwelling'. That is a third thing to rule on and it is the
reason this is not a one-line fix.
