# D. Weaver's building — Lot 2 or Lot 9, block 1, North Water street

**Question (T-0328).** Two printings of the same advertisement gave two different lot
numbers, and a lot number is as close to a map reference as the newspaper corpus ever
gets: it is the difference between a documented building standing at one place on the
north-bank river frontage and standing at another.

**Answer. Lot 2.** Four printings to one, on four separate settings of the type.

## The witnesses

The notice is a standing advertisement, dated in its own copy to November 1834 and run
in five consecutive numbers of the *Chicago Democrat*. All five stand on issue page 3;
four of them in column 3 and the last in column 4.

| issue | page/col | claim | the line, verbatim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1834-11-26 | 3 / 3 | `c010` | `ling on Lot 2, in block 1, on North` |
| 1834-12-03 | 3 / 3 | `c025` | `SuE building on Lot 2, in block 1, on North` |
| 1834-12-10 | 3 / 3 | `c012` | `on Lot 2, in block 1, on North` |
| **1834-12-17** | 3 / 3 | `c006` | `building on Lot 9, in block 1, on Norh` |
| 1834-12-24 | 3 / 4 | `c012` | `THE building on Lot 2, in block 1, on Nort` |

Only the last two had been claimed before T-0328. The November read (T-0293) and the
first half of the December read (T-0294) passed over the notice — in 1834-11-26 and
1834-12-10 it survives inside an alternating pair of physical columns, woven line by
line, which is the shape those reads found hardest.

## Why this is evidence and not a tidy-up

T-0294 claimed both of the printings it found, each quoting its own digit, and amended
neither to agree with the other. That was right, and this reading does not undo it:
**no transcription has been edited.** What has changed is the number of witnesses.

The notice is **reset weekly** — its line breaks, its capitals, its dateline and even
its column all move between numbers — so the four agreeing printings are four
compositors' readings of Weaver's copy and four passes of the transcriber over
different type, not one reading counted four times. That is the whole warrant here: a
majority of independent settings, not a manufactured agreement between two.

## The losing column, on its own record

1834-12-17 page 3 column 3 is the least reliable of the five, and demonstrably so from
inside its own three lines:

- it sets `Norh` for *North*;
- it loses the word `Water` to the alternating column entirely;
- it mangles the advertisement's dateline to `3, Nor. 13, 131` — **misreading the 9 of
  `Nov. 19` as a 3**, in the very lines it is supposed to be reading a 9 in.

A column that misreads a 9 as a 3 one line below is not the witness to prefer on
whether a 2 is a 9.

## What is still open

**The dateline's day does not agree across the printings.** 1834-11-26 and 1834-12-03
read `Nov. 12`, 1834-12-24 reads `Nov. 19`, 1834-12-17 reads `Nor. 13`, and
1834-12-10's is unreadable. The two claims that predate T-0328 carry
`ad_copy_date: 1834-11-19`; the three T-0328 adds assert no `ad_copy_date` at all
rather than pick a side inside a ticket that was not opened for it. Filed as its own
ticket.

## Status of the reading

`transcription_mediated`, not `scan_verified`. T-0328's acceptance asked for the lot
number to be read off a page image; **the page images are not in the repository** —
the deposit at `chicago/reference/newspapers/` holds transcriptions only. The digit is
settled here on the corpus's own text, which is a weaker instrument than a scan and a
stronger one than the two-witness deadlock it replaces. A scan read would still
outrank it, as `data/sources/chicago_democrat_1833_11_26.json` outranks the
transcription for the first number.

## What it means for the model

`block 1, North Water street` is inside the modelled town, on the north bank at the
east end of the frontage. Nothing stands for this building yet; placing it is
T-0263 / T-0306 territory. When it is placed, **Lot 2**.
