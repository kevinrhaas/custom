# The owner's brief of 2026-08-18 — reference views, and a standing ruling

Eleven reference images supplied by the project owner in the session of 2026-08-18,
plus one screenshot of the current render. **The image binaries are not yet committed** —
they were supplied in-session and this file is the written record of what each shows,
made so the queue tickets that consume them (T-0062 … T-0079) are self-contained.
Most are identifiable published plates; T-0075 owns identifying each against
chicagology/Andreas plate numbering and creating proper source records, after which
the binaries (or better scans of the same plates) belong in this directory.

**These are TIER 5 PICTORIAL sources** unless a record says otherwise — retrospective
views drawn decades after 1835. They may drive **massing, roof form, fenestration,
materials, furniture and setting** as `inferred`, and they may never drive a coordinate
or a footprint outline. Several depict people: **L1 stands — the people in these images
are reference for scale and use only and are never depicted in the scene.**

## The standing ruling that came with them

The owner, verbatim: *"in general i think you are being too cautious on adding items to
the scene. you are totally fine to be liberal with adding reconstructed items when i ask
for things, you can just label and mark them as such."* This extends the 2026-08-15
ruling in AGENTS.md § RECONSTRUCTED IS A TIER and is now recorded there. Where a ticket
below quotes the owner overriding a conservative reading (two attested docks, signs only
where attested, wagons only at attested yards), the override is the rule.

## The images

1. **The first Cook County jail** (engraving; Andreas-family plate). A low log jail —
   squared timber, near-flat roof — behind it and along the street: **board fences lining
   the street frontage and plank sidewalks** at the fence line. Consumed by T-0070 (the
   jail itself) and T-0069 (the streetscape it demonstrates).
2. **The Dearborn Street drawbridge, view one** (engraving). The 1834 **gallows-frame
   drawbridge**: twin timber A-frames/gallows posts above the deck, chains to the leaves,
   timber approaches. **Sailing vessels (schooner rig) moored close by**; small boats on
   the water. Consumed by T-0071 and T-0063.
3. **The Dearborn Street drawbridge, view two** (engraving). Same bridge from farther
   out: masts crowd the reach below the bridge; a light/beacon structure near the mouth;
   low warehouses on the banks. Consumed by T-0071, T-0063, T-0062.
4. **The Wolf Tavern** (engraving). A one-and-a-half-storey **log** tavern, and beside it
   a **very tall pole with a cross-arm at the top, flying a board sign painted with a
   wolf** — read by the owner as "almost like a flag". Barrels by the wall. Consumed by
   T-0072.
5. **The Tremont House street scene, 1833** (the dense street view; owner reads it as
   **looking southwest from the corner of Lake and Dearborn**). A continuous two-storey
   storefront row: shared party lines, signboards across the fronts, awnings, plank
   walks. The owner: *"you can reconstruct most of the main downtown area from this
   diagram. there should be more and denser buildings. this is important."* Consumed by
   T-0077/T-0078/T-0079 (the T-0073 split) and T-0066.
6. **The Green Tree Inn — 1838** (Braunhold engraving, Andreas 1884; the existing
   2026-08-11 set holds a sibling as plate "11"). Two-storey clapboard inn, even 6-over-6
   bays, end chimneys; **post-mounted hanging signboard** at the corner; **plank sidewalks
   with board crossings**; a lower gabled wing at the left with a wide (carriage) door;
   **ship masts rise directly behind the roofline** — the river is that close. Consumed by
   T-0042, T-0066, T-0069.
7. **The Green Tree Inn** (Trowbridge drawing). Same inn from the other quarter: the
   hanging "GREEN TREE" sign on its post, a **bench of sitters against the front wall**,
   an **open-sided wagon shed attached at the left with a covered wagon under it**, farm
   wagons in the yard, a dirt road with **grass verges**, brush and small trees at the
   fence lines. Consumed by T-0042, T-0064, T-0067.
8. **"Sauganash Hotel. 1831"** (C. E. Petford watercolour, Chicago History Museum
   family). The clapboard two-storey block with its **log wing** at the left; **green
   (moss/shingle-dark) roof**, **brick chimneys**; a small flat-hooded **entrance
   frontispiece/porch** on the front door; **plank sidewalk with a board crossing over
   the road**; two **posts** (hitching/corner posts) at the road edge; a **vertical-board
   fence** running off to the right; trees behind the fence. Consumed by T-0043, T-0069.
9. **"The Sauganash Hotel"** (Braunhold engraving, © A. T. Andreas 1884). Same building:
   log wing with its shed-roofed porch over the door, plank walks on both frontages,
   **hitching posts**, board fence at the right, the clapboard block's wide entrance
   surround. Consumed by T-0043.
10. **The Sauganash** (Trowbridge drawing). Adds: **louvred shutters on the sash**, the
    tall board fence enclosing the rear yard, a saddled horse at the post (L1: reference
    only), the log wing's door direct to grade. Consumed by T-0043.
11. **"South Water Street in 1834 — now Wacker Drive"** (© Chicago Historical Society
    postcard of a retrospective painting). The **south bank river frontage as a
    continuous working row**: roughly ten one-storey log and frame buildings shoulder to
    shoulder facing the river, two two-storey frame stores anchoring the east end; an
    **ox-drawn covered wagon train** on the river street; **rowboats** on the water and
    at the bank; a single large tree at the west; grassy bank between street and water.
    Consumed by T-0078, T-0062, T-0063, T-0064.
12. **Chicago circa 1833, looking east** (coloured retrospective view; lake on the
    horizon). Scattered frame houses, and the ground plane is the lesson: **split-rail
    and board fences line both sides of the roads and enclose every property**; inside
    the fences the ground reads as **garden, orchard rows, dooryard green — not prairie**;
    **trees and bushes stand close around the houses**, kept deliberately; a covered
    wagon on the road. Consumed by T-0068, T-0067, T-0069, T-0074.

Plus: **the owner's screenshot of the current render** at Dearborn & Lake (the Tremont
House card open) — the corner that image 5 depicts, rendered today as a lone building on
open grass. That gap is T-0077's whole brief.

### What each identification rests on

**3 — the drawbridge.** The plate at `chicagology.com/harbor/riverbridges/dearbornstreetbridges/`
carries the three things the brief gives image 3 and not image 2: masts crowding the reach
below the bridge, a tapering light structure on the far shore near the mouth, and low
warehouses along the bank. It also draws the bridge as image 2 describes it — twin gallows
frames, chains to the leaves, a railed deck. **Image 2 is a different plate and is not
claimed by this record.**

**5 — the Tremont row.** The signature was read off the plate at 5× (`N. Roswell Gifford`);
the row, the party lines, the lettered boards and the board walk are all on it. **Awnings are
not**, at the resolution published, and the record says so rather than nodding the brief
through.

**7 — the Green Tree.** Every feature the brief lists is on the plate: the GREEN TREE board
on its post, the bench of sitters, the attached wagon shed with a covered wagon under it, the
farm wagons, the grass verges, the brush at the fence lines. Signed and dated by the artist.

**10 — the Sauganash.** Louvred shutters, the tall board fence behind, the saddled horse at a
post, the log wing's door direct to grade — all four, plus the road-edge posts. The signature
carries a two-digit year that is **not legible** at this resolution, so the record dates it
"undated" and says the Green Tree companion is signed 1902.

### What was searched for the other eight, and where to look next

- **1 (jail), 4 (Wolf Tavern), 11 (South Water 1834), 12 (Chicago c. 1833).** Chicagology's own
  search was run for each subject; it returned topic pages (`prefire439` Cook County Jail,
  `prefire273` Wolf's Point, the South Water Street street page) and **none of those pages
  carries the plate described.** The jail page in particular reproduces no engraving at all.
- **6 and 9 (the two Braunhold engravings).** Both are stated in the brief to be Andreas 1884
  plates, and `docs/RESEARCH/sauganash_hotel.md` § 4 already reads image 9 as such. Neither is
  reproduced on the Chicagology pages this project holds records for. **The next attempt should
  go to Andreas volume I at page-image level** (the same fetch `docs/LIBERTIES.md` names as the
  resolution for several open liberties) rather than to another aggregator: a plate cited to a
  page of the volume is worth more than the same plate cited to a blog, and it settles the
  rights question at the same time.
- **8 (the Petford watercolour).** Attributed in the brief to the Chicago History Museum
  family. A museum object needs its accession number, and that is a catalogue lookup rather
  than a web search; `chm_green_tree_1859` (ICHi-040230) is the shape of the record it should
  become.
- **11 (the CHS postcard).** Carries a © line in the brief's own description, so it is
  `check_required` at best and quite possibly `restricted`. It should not be given a record
  until the rights line is read off the object.

**None of the eight blocks anything.** Every claim they support is already committed as
`inferred` or `reconstructed` with the brief's path in its note, which is exactly what a
citation-by-path is for. What the four new records change is that the claims resting on
*them* now cite a plate with an artist, a locator and an archived copy behind it.
