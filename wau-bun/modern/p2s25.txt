Mr. Kinzie, as has been told, joined his family at Detroit in the month of January. Not long after, General Proctor began to suspect that he was in correspondence with General Harrison, who was now at Fort Meigs and was believed to be planning a move on Detroit. Lieutenant Watson of the British army called on Mr. Kinzie one day with an invitation to General Proctor's quarters on the other side of the river, saying the General wished to speak with him on business. Suspecting nothing, he accepted, and to his surprise was ordered into confinement and kept under strict guard in the house of his former partner, Mr. Patterson of Sandwich. When he did not come home, Mrs. Kinzie told some of the Indian chiefs who were his particular friends, and they went straight to the commanding officer's head-quarters, demanded "their friend's" release, and brought him back to his house. The General waited a while until a favorable opportunity came, and then sent a detachment of dragoons to arrest Mr. Kinzie. They succeeded in carrying him off, and were crossing the river with him when a party of friendly Indians appeared.

"Where is the Shaw-nee-aw-kee?" was their first question.

"There," his wife answered, pointing across the river, "in the hands of the red-coats, who are taking him away again."

The Indians ran down to the river, seized some canoes they found there, crossed over to Sandwich, and forced General Proctor to give up his intentions a second time.

A third time the officer tried it, and this time he succeeded in arresting Mr. Kinzie and carrying him, heavily ironed, to Fort Malden in Canada, at the mouth of the Detroit River. At first he was treated there with great severity, but after a while his confinement was eased somewhat and he was allowed to walk along the bank of the river for air and exercise.
