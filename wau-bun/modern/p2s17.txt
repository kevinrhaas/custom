One soldier's wife showed a heroic resolution that deserves to be recorded. She was a Mrs. Corbin, and from the first she had said she would never let herself fall into the hands of the savages, believing that their prisoners were always put to tortures worse than death.

So when a party came up to take her prisoner, she fought them desperately and refused to surrender, even though they made signs to her that she would be safe and well treated. She literally let herself be cut to pieces rather than become their captive.

There was a Sergeant Holt who took a ball in the neck early in the engagement. Finding himself badly wounded, he gave his sword to his wife, who was on horseback beside him, and told her to defend herself; then he made for the lake to get out of the way of the balls. Mrs. Holt was riding a very fine horse that the Indians wanted to have, so they went at her hoping to get her off it.

They fought only with the butt-ends of their guns, since their object was not to kill her. She hacked and hewed at the pieces as they were thrust at her, first on one side, then the other. At last she broke loose from them and dashed off into the prairie. The Indians chased her, shouting and laughing and calling out now and then, "The brave woman! Don't hurt her!"

Eventually they overtook her again, and while she was busy with two or three of them in front, one of them managed to seize her by the back of the neck and drag her off her horse, big and powerful woman that she was. Even though she had hacked their guns about badly and cut some of them severely, they seemed to regard her with nothing but admiration. They took her to a trader on the Illinois River, who returned her to her friends; she had been shown every kindness while she was a captive.
