We were not left in suspense for long about the friends we had left behind. In less than two weeks Old Smoker turned up again. He was carrying letters from my husband, which told me that General Dodge was with him at Fort Winnebago just then, that Generals Henry and Alexander were at the Fort as well, and that as soon as they had rested their men and horses — pretty well worn out from scouring the country after Black Hawk — they would march again in pursuit of him toward the head-waters of the Rock River, where everything the Winnebagoes had lately brought in gave them good reason to think he would be found.

Since he told us not to worry about him at all, and also held out the hope of coming for us, or sending for us, soon, our minds settled down considerably.

Not long after that, I was told one morning that "a lady" wanted to see me at the front door. I went out, and to my surprise it was my friend Madame Four-Legs. After a great show of delight at seeing me — putting her two hands together above her forehead and then parting them in a kind of waving motion, laughing, and patting my arms — she pulled out from inside her dress a letter from my husband that she had brought. It read like this: "Generals Dodge and Henry left here a few days since, accompanied by Paquette; they met the Sauks near the Wisconsin, on the 21st. A battle ensued, in which upwards of fifty of the enemy were killed — our loss was one killed, and eight wounded. The citizens are well pleased that all this has been accomplished without any aid from Old White Beaver. The war must be near its close, for the militia and regulars together will soon finish the remaining handful of fugitives."
