As the troops left the fort, the band struck up the Dead March. On they came, in military order, but with grave faces. Captain Wells led the way at the head of his little band of Miamis. Before leaving the garrison he had blackened his face, as a sign that he knew what was coming to him. They took their route along the lake shore. When they reached the point where a range of sand-hills began, running between the prairie and the beach, the escort of Pottowattamies — about five hundred of them — kept to the level of the prairie instead of staying down on the beach with the Americans and the Miamis.
