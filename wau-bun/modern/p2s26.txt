On the 10th of September, while he was taking his walk under the close supervision of a guard of soldiers, the whole party was startled by the sound of guns out on Lake Erie, not far below them. What could it mean? It had to be Commodore Barclay firing into some of the Yankees. The firing went on. The time allowed the prisoner for his daily walk ran out, but neither he nor his guard noticed it go by, they were listening so anxiously to what they now felt sure was an engagement between ships of war. At last Mr. Kinzie was reminded that the hour had come for him to go back into confinement. He asked for another half-hour.

"Let me stay," he said, "till we can learn how the battle has gone."

Very soon a sloop came round the point under a press of sail, and then two gun-boats after her in chase.

"She's running — she has the British colors up," he cried. "Yes, yes, they are coming down — she's striking her flag! Now," he said, turning to the soldiers, "I'll go back to prison content. I know how the battle has gone."

The sloop was the Little Belt, the last of the squadron taken by the gallant Perry on that memorable occasion, which he announced in the immortal words:

"We have met the enemy, and they are ours!"
