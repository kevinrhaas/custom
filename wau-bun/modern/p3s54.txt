When the announcement came at last that "the boats were in sight," it was a thrilling and most joyful sound.

Hundreds of poor creatures gathered on the bank to watch them arrive. How agonizing it was to see how slowly they came on, following the winding course of the river across the wide prairie! As the first boat touched land, we — who were watching the scene with an anxiety and impatience matched only by the sufferers' own — could hardly keep from laughing at the sight of old Wild-Cat, who had fallen off somewhat in his enormous bulk, seizing "the Washington Woman" in his arms and hugging her and dancing with her in the ecstasy of his delight.

Their Father made a sign to them all to go to work with the hatchets they had been holding ready for a long time, and in an unbelievably short time barrel after barrel of corn was broken open and emptied. Even the little children got hold of pans and kettles full and hurried off to the fires blazing all around, to parch and cook what they had seized.

From that time on there was no more destitution. This sudden plenty was followed almost at once by the arrival of supplies for the Commissary's Department, and, fed and strengthened again, our poor children went back to their villages to get their crops ready for the coming season.
