It seems that after the Indians' first attack the Americans charged the ones who had hidden themselves in a kind of ravine lying between the sand-banks and the prairie. The Indians pulled together into a body, and after some hard fighting — by which time the whites were down to twenty-eight men — this little band managed to break through the enemy and reach rising ground not far from the Oak Woods. Any further fighting now looked hopeless, so Lieutenant Helm sent Peresh Leclerc, a half-breed boy in Mr. Kinzie's service who had come along with the detachment and fought manfully on their side, to propose terms of surrender. It was agreed that the lives of all the survivors would be spared and that they could be ransomed as soon as it could be managed.

But in the meantime something horrible had happened. One young savage climbed into the baggage-wagon that held the children of the white families, twelve of them, and tomahawked every one of them. This was during the engagement near the sand-hills. When Captain Wells, who was fighting close by, saw it, he cried out, "Is that their game, butchering the women and children? Then I will kill, too!"

With that he turned his horse's head and set off for the Indian camp near the fort, where their own women and children had been left.

Several Indians came after him as he galloped along. He lay flat on his horse's neck, loading and firing in that position, and sometimes turning on his pursuers. In the end their balls told: they killed his horse and wounded him badly. At that moment Winnemeg and Wau-ban-see came up and tried to save him from the men who had now caught up with him. They got him clear of the horse and were helping him along when another Indian, Pee-so-tum, gave him his death-blow, stabbing him in the back.
