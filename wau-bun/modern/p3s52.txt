One day while I was doing this, a face I had once known well appeared at the window. It was the pretty daughter of the elder Day-kau-ray. She had often visited us before, watching what we did with great interest — our sewing, our weeding and working in the garden, our reading. I had tried many times to give her some idea of reading, showing her the plates in the Family Bible and doing my best to explain them to her, but lately I had quite lost sight of her. Now, how changed she was, how thin and worn she looked! When I spoke to her with my usual phrase, "Tshah-ko-zhah?" (What is it?), she gave a sigh that was almost a sob. She did not beg, but her face said everything.

I took my dish and handed it to her, expecting to see her eat what was in it greedily. But no — she took it, made a sign that she would be back soon, and walked away. When she brought it back, I was almost sure she had not tasted a mouthful herself.

Oh, the boats — the boats with the corn! Why did they not come? We both wrote and sent messages to hurry them, but everything and everybody moved so slowly in those unenterprising days. All we could be sure of was that they would come when they were ready and not a moment before.
