On the afternoon of the 7th of August, Winnemeg — or Catfish — a Pottowattamie chief, reached the post with despatches from General Hull. They announced that war had been declared between the United States and Great Britain, that General Hull had reached Detroit at the head of the Northwestern army, and that the island of Mackinac had fallen into British hands.

Captain Heald's orders were "to evacuate the fort, if practicable, and, in that event, to distribute all the United States' property contained in the fort, and in the United States' factory or agency, among the Indians in the neighborhood."

Once he had handed over the despatches, Winnemeg asked to speak privately with Mr. Kinzie, who had moved into the fort. He told Mr. Kinzie that he knew what the messages he had carried said, and asked him to find out whether Captain Heald meant to abandon the post. He argued strongly against any such step, on the grounds that the garrison was well supplied with ammunition and had provisions enough for six months. It would be far better, he thought, to stay where they were until reinforcements could be sent up to help them. But if Captain Heald did decide to leave, then he should go immediately. The Pottowattamies whose country they would have to cross did not yet know what Winnemeg's errand had been, so a forced march might get the troops through before the ones who felt hostile were ready to stop them.

Captain Heald was told at once about this advice, and how earnestly it had been given. He answered that he did intend to abandon the post, but that since his orders were to distribute the United States' property, he would not feel justified in going until he had gathered the Indians of the neighborhood and divided it fairly among them.

Winnemeg then suggested that the wiser course would be to march out and leave everything standing just as it was. It was possible that while the Indians were busy dividing up the spoils, the troops might make their retreat without being touched at all. Mr. Kinzie seconded this advice strongly, but it did not meet with the commanding officer's approval.
