It was nearly dark when we got to Lawton's. The Aux Plaines was frozen over, and the house stood on the far side. We shouted until a man came out of the building, and he managed to cut through the ice and bring a canoe across to us — but by the time he did, it had grown hard to make anything out in the dark.

Lawton's turned out to be a very comfortable house, once we finally reached it. Carpets on the floor, a warm stove — altogether quite civilized. Mr. Weeks, the man who had brought us across, was running the place while Mr. Lawton was away.

Mrs. Lawton was a young woman, and not bad-looking. She complained bitterly about how lonely her life was, and about having been "brought out here into the woods, which was not at all what she had expected when she came from the East." We did not ask her what exactly she had expected on coming to a wild, unsettled country. We tried instead to comfort her by saying that things would improve in a few years. She said "she did not intend to wait that long. She would go back to her family in the East, unless Mr. Lawton invited some of her young friends out to stay with her and make the place agreeable."
