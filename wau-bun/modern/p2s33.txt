After a hard, painful march that took many days, the party reached the Seneca village on the head-waters of the Alleghany, near what is now called Olean Point. When they arrived, the chief who had brought them, who went by the name of the Big White Man, led his prisoners to the principal lodge. It belonged to his mother, the widow of the head chief of that band, and the one they called the Old Queen.

Once in her presence, her son presented the little girl to her and said, "My mother, I bring you a child to take the place of my brother, who was killed by the Lenape six moons ago. She shall dwell in my lodge and be a sister to me. Take the white woman and her children and treat them kindly — our father will give us many horses and guns to buy them back again."

By "our father" he meant the British Indian Agent for his tribe, Colonel Johnson, an excellent and benevolent gentleman who lived at Fort Niagara, on the British side of the river of that name.

The old queen did exactly as her son had told her. She took the prisoners in, and they were given every comfort that her simple, primitive way of living made possible.
