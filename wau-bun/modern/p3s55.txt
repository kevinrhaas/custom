That spring we had a visit from the Rev. Mr. Kent and Mrs. Kent, of Galena. The event is memorable, because it was the first time the gospel was preached at Fort Winnebago according to the Protestant faith. The large parlor of the hospital was fitted up for the service, and we said to one another gladly, "Let us go to the house of the Lord!"

For nearly three years we had lived here without the blessing of a public service of praise and thanksgiving. We took this beginning as a sign of better times, and our little "sewing-society" worked with fresh energy to raise a fund that might one day pay for the permanent services of a missionary.

Not long after this, on a fine spring morning, while we were sitting at breakfast, a party of Indians came into the parlor and up to the door of the room where we were. Two of them passed through and went out onto a small portico; the third stayed standing in the doorway where he had first appeared. He was almost directly opposite me, and when I looked up I knew him at once, in spite of his change of dress and the paint he was covered with.

I went on pouring the coffee, and as I did it I said to my husband, "The one behind you, the one you are talking to, is one of the escaped prisoners."

Without turning his head, Mr. Kinzie went on listening to all their instructions about the repairing of their guns and traps and so on, which they wanted to leave with the blacksmith. As they went on, he turned carelessly toward the parlor door and answered the one who was speaking to him. When he spoke to me again, it was to say, "You are right, but it is no affair of ours. None of us is to look in any way that would give him notice that we suspect anything. They are undoubtedly innocent, and they have suffered enough already."

Contrary to his usual custom, their Father did not ask their names. He wrote out their directions, tied them to the various implements, and told them to go and deliver them to M. Morrin themselves.

The rest of our circle were hugely pleased at the young fellow's audacity, and we rather longed to tell the officers that we could have caught one of their fugitives for them, if we had had a mind to.
