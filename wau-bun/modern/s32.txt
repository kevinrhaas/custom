Between small incidents of this kind, and the occasional dinner or tea party for the young officers — sometimes at the Major's quarters, sometimes at ours — our life went along pleasantly enough. Now and then I would amuse myself by making something very nice, a fruit cake or a pie, to send over to the young officers' quarters as a present, on the theory that with no lady to preside over their mess, it might now and then run short of that sort of thing. Mrs. Twiggs was lucky enough to have well-trained servants to do for her what, thanks to my little dark handmaid, always ended up falling to me.

One day I made some mince pies, which the Major and my husband both thought very highly of, and I decided to send one to each of the young officers.

It happened that on that same day my husband, coming back from overseeing his men on the other side of the river, had some errand that took him to Captain Harney's quarters.

Dinner had just been put on the table, and the Captain would not hear of his visitor doing anything but sitting down and eating with him and another gentleman who was there. The pork and beans were declared excellent, and when they had been dealt with, a mince pie followed.

The Captain cut it and helped his guests, then took a piece himself and started to taste it. He shoved his plate back with a jerk and an exclamation, and called out to his servant, a little thickset mulatto who was waiting on them: "David, you yellow rascal, how dare you put such a pie on my table?" Then, turning apologetically to the company, he said, "If there's one thing on earth David does understand, it's how to make a mince pie — and here he has gone and filled this one with brandy, so we can't eat a mouthful of it!"

"Please, sir," said David modestly, "I didn't make the pie. It's one Mrs. Kinzie sent over as a present."

The poor Captain was now in a fix. He raged at himself, and at the same time begged my husband in the most earnest terms not to tell me what a mistake he had made — an instruction that went straight out of my husband's head the moment he got home. As for the unlucky Captain, he did not dare call on me again until he felt sure I had forgotten the whole business.
