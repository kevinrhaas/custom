What we had long expected in the way of suffering among the Indians began to show itself as the spring came on. We first learned how far it went from the ones who came in in little parties, begging for food.

As long as it was possible to issue an occasional ration, their Father went on doing it, but the supplies in the Commissary Department were now so far reduced that Colonel Cutler did not feel he could authorize anything beyond the barest relief, and that only in extreme cases.

All winter we had been as economical as we possibly could with our own stores, so as not to use up our thin stock of flour and meal before it could be replenished from "below." We had even bought some sour flour that the commissary had condemned, and had managed, with plenty of saleratus and a fair proportion of potatoes, to make it into a very palatable kind of bread. But we had gone on giving to party after party as they came to tell us they were starving, and the time finally came when we had nothing left to give.

The half-breed families of the neighborhood, who like us had gone on sharing with the hungry as long as their own supplies held out, were now forced to refuse any more help. These women often came to grieve with us over the terrible news that came in from the wintering grounds. It had been a very open winter. There had hardly been enough snow at any time for the Indians to track the deer — and in fact the game had all been driven off by the troops and the war-parties that had swept the country the summer before.

We heard of them dying by whole companies of nothing but starvation, and lying stretched out along the road to the Portage, which they had been trying to drag their worn-out bodies down. Soup made from the bark of the slippery elm, or stewed acorns, was all that many of them had lived on for weeks.

For a long time we had been getting our own food in daily rations from the garrison, since things had reached the point where there was no way to get a whole barrel of flour at once. After our meals were finished I always went into the pantry and carefully collected every last particle of food that was left and set it aside, to be given to one of the wretched applicants who crowded around us constantly.
