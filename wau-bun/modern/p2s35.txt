Time went on, and every year the hope of recovering the little captive grew fainter. Meanwhile she was winding herself closer and closer around the heart of her Indian brother. Nothing could have exceeded the consideration and affection she was shown, not only by him but by his mother, the Old Queen. Their whole stock of brooches and wampum went into decorating her. The best seat and the choicest food were always kept for her, and no effort was spared to make her happy and to make her forget her old home and her own people.

So although she had watched her parents and her dear little brother go away with a feeling that came close to despair, and had for a long time refused every attempt to console her, preferring death to a life cut off from everyone she loved, time did what time always does. It brought its soothing balm, and in the end she grew contented and happy.

Because she was so active and had such energy of character — qualities she was known for to the very last period of her life — they gave her the name The Ship under full sail.
