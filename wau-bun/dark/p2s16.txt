It appears that when the first attack came, the Americans went straight at the men who had hidden themselves in the kind of ravine that runs between the sand-banks and the open prairie. Those men drew together into one body, and the fighting that followed was hard. By the end of it there were twenty-eight whites left alive. Those twenty-eight forced a way through and got up onto rising ground a short distance from the Oak Woods. To go on fighting was plainly useless. Lieutenant Helm sent out Peresh Leclerc, a half-breed boy in the service of Mr. Kinzie who had come along with the detachment and had fought manfully on their side, to ask for terms. The terms agreed were that every survivor should keep his life, and that they might be ransomed as soon as it could be arranged.

While that was being settled, something else had already been done. Back near the sand-hills, in the middle of the engagement, one young savage had climbed up into the baggage-wagon where the children of the white families were sitting. There were twelve of them. He killed all twelve with his tomahawk. Captain Wells was fighting close enough to see it happen, and he called out, - "Is that their game, butchering the women and children? Then I will kill, too!"

He pulled his horse's head round as he said it and rode for the Indian camp beside the fort, where their own squaws and children had been left.

Several Indians came after him. He went down flat along his horse's neck and rode that way, loading and firing from that position, and twisting round now and then to fire at the men behind him. In the end their balls found him. The horse was killed and he himself was badly wounded. Winnemeg and Wau-ban-see reached him then and tried to keep him alive; they got him clear of the dead animal and were helping him along between them when another Indian, Pee-so-tum, came up behind him and stabbed him in the back, and that was the blow that killed him.
