When the announcement came at last — that the boats were in sight — it was a thrilling and a most joyful sound.

Hundreds of poor creatures gathered on the bank to watch them arrive. It was torture to see how slowly they came, following the winding course of the river through the whole width of that prairie. We were watching with an anxiety and impatience matched only by that of the sufferers themselves; and yet, when the first boat touched the land, we could scarcely keep from laughing, because old Wild-Cat — who had fallen off considerably from his huge amount of flesh — seized "the Washington Woman" in his arms and hugged her and danced her about in the ecstasy of his delight.

Their Father made a sign for them all to fall to with their hatchets, which they had been holding ready a long while, and in an incredibly short time barrel after barrel of corn was broken open and emptied out. Even the little children got themselves pans and kettles full of it and hurried away to the fires blazing all around, to parch and cook what they had seized.

From that time there was no more destitution. The present abundance was followed immediately by the arrival of supplies for the Commissary's Department, and, refreshed and strengthened again, our poor children went back to their villages to get their crops ready for the coming season.
