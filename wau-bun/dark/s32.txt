Little incidents of that kind, and now and then a dinner or a tea-party for the young officers — sometimes at the Major's quarters, sometimes at ours — carried our life along pleasantly enough. Now and again I amused myself by making something very nice, a fruit cake or a pie, and sending it over to the young officers' quarters as a present, on the theory that a mess with no lady to preside over it might run short of that sort of thing. Mrs. Twiggs had well-trained servants to do for her what, thanks to my little dark handmaid, always came back around to me.

One day I made some mince pies. The Major and my husband both approved of them highly, and I thought I would send one to each of the young officers.

It happened that my husband, coming back that same day from superintending his men on the other side of the river, had an errand that took him to Captain Harney's quarters.

Dinner had just gone on the table. The Captain would not hear of his leaving, and sat him down with himself and another gentleman who was there. The pork and beans were pronounced excellent. When they were cleared away, a mince pie came on.

The Captain cut it and helped his guests, then took a piece himself and began to taste it. He pushed his plate back with a jerk and an exclamation, and called to his servant, a little thick-set mulatto who waited on him. "David, you yellow rascal, how dare you put such a pie on my table?" Then he turned to the company to apologize. "If there is anything on earth David does understand, it is how to make a mince pie, and here he has filled this with brandy, so we can't eat a morsel of it!"

"Please, sir," said David, modestly, "I didn't make the pie — it is one Mrs. Kinzie sent as a present."

That left the poor Captain nowhere to stand. He raved at himself in front of them all, and begged my husband in the most earnest terms not to tell me what a mistake he had made — a request that went straight out of my husband's head the moment he was inside his own door. As for the unlucky Captain, he did not venture to call on me again until he was quite sure the whole circumstance had gone out of mine.
