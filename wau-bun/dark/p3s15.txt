She came one day with a half-grown boy behind her, and the boy was carrying a young fawn she had brought me for a present. I was charmed with it — the soft eyes, the dappled coat — but I had heard people say "as wild as a fawn" all my life and I did not expect to get anywhere with taming it. To my complete surprise, it learned in no time to follow me like a dog. Wherever I went, Fan went. At breakfast she would lie down under the table across my feet. One of her earliest gestures of affection was to chew every bit of trimming off my black silk apron while pretending to nuzzle and fuss over me. That was not the only kind of mischief in her, either.

One day there was a great clattering of crockery from the kitchen. We ran in to see what had happened and found that Miss Fan had climbed onto a shelf of the dresser, about two feet off the floor, and was working out how to lie down comfortably among the plates and dishes. It did not take me long to see that what she wanted was the shelf above her head — that she always looked for a place to sleep under a chair, or under anything that made a roof of sorts over her. So after that, when her morning nap was coming on, I would open a big green parasol and stand it on the matting in the corner, and when I called "Fan, Fan," she would come and settle in under it and go straight to sleep.

One morning Fan was gone. We called for her and hunted the garden, the cattle enclosure, the Frenchmen's houses, the whole length of the hill toward Paquette's. No Fan anywhere. We decided her own wild nature had finally spoken and she had gone off to the woods.

It was a hot forenoon and every door in the house stood open. Around dinner-time Fan came in at a dead run, panting so hard her whole body worked with it, and threw herself down on her side with her legs stretched straight out and her mouth foaming, and everything about her said she was dying. We tried to give her water. We tried to gentle her, in case it was only fright that had her like that. In a few minutes she gave a gasp, and a spasm went through her, and she was dead. Whether the greyhounds had run her, or whether she had eaten some poisonous weed and the pain of it had sent her back to the people she trusted most, we never found out. We only knew we had lost our pretty pet, and a good many tears were shed over her.
