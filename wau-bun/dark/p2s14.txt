They had gone perhaps a mile and a half when Captain Wells, who had ridden out in front with his Miamis, came back down the line at a gallop.

"They are about to attack us," he shouted, "form instantly, and charge upon them."

He had barely got the words out. The volley came down on them from among the sand-hills. The troops were pushed into line where they stood and sent up the bank at a charge, and one of them — a veteran of seventy winters — went down on the way up. What happened after that is better told by somebody who stood in the middle of it: Mrs. Helm, wife of Captain Helm, who was a lieutenant then, and step-daughter of Mr. Kinzie.

"After we left the bank, everyone was firing. The Miamis were gone at the first shot. Their chief turned his horse toward the Pottowattamies and spoke to them:"

"'You have deceived the Americans and us. You have done a bad action, and (brandishing his tomahawk) I will be the first to head a party of Americans to return and punish your treachery.' Then he was gone after his own people, who were already streaking away over the prairies."

"The troops fought gallantly. There was only a handful of them, and they seemed to have decided to make the price of their lives as high as they could. Our horses would not stand; they pranced and bounded and could scarcely be held while the balls went by them. I pulled off a little way and looked at my husband and my father, both of them still untouched. I understood that my own hour had come. I tried to put every person I loved out of my mind, and to make myself ready for what was coming."

"While I was doing that, the surgeon, Dr. Van Voorhees, came up to me. He was badly hurt. His horse had been shot out from under him and he had taken a ball in the leg, and every muscle of his face was moving with the agony of terror. 'Do you think they will take our lives? I am badly wounded, but I think not mortally. Maybe we might purchase our lives by promising them a large reward. Do you think there is any chance?'"

"'Dr. Van Voorhees,' I said, 'don't let us waste the few moments that yet stay to us in such vain hopes. Our fate is inevitable. In a few moments we must appear before the bar of God. Let us make what preparation is yet in our power.'"

"'Oh, I can't die!' he exclaimed, 'I am not fit to die - if I had but a short time to prepare - death is awful!'"

"Ensign Ronan was close by us, hurt past saving, almost on the ground, and still fighting on one knee with everything left in him.[35] I pointed at him. 'Look at that man!' I said. 'At least he dies like a soldier.'"

"'Yes,' he answered, and the word came out of him as a convulsive gasp, 'but he has no terrors of the future - he is an unbeliever!'"

"Then a young Indian brought a tomahawk down at me. I threw myself sideways, so that the blade meant for my skull landed on my shoulder instead. I got both arms around his neck. His scalping-knife hung in a scabbard over his breast, and I was working with everything I had to get hold of it when an older man took me and dragged me out of his grasp."
