They had marched perhaps a mile and a half when Captain Wells, who had gone somewhat ahead with his Miamis, came riding furiously back down the line.

"They are about to attack us," he shouted. "Form instantly, and charge upon them."

The words were barely out of him when the volley came down out of the sand-hills. The troops were brought hastily into line and charged up the bank. One man, a veteran of seventy winters, fell going up. The rest of it is best told by someone who was inside it: Mrs. Helm, the wife of Captain — then Lieutenant — Helm, and step-daughter of Mr. Kinzie.

"After we had left the bank the firing became general. The Miamis fled at the outset. Their chief rode up to the Pottowattamies, and said:

"'You have deceived the Americans and us. You have done a bad action, and — ' brandishing his tomahawk — 'I will be the first to head a party of Americans to return and punish your treachery.' Then he galloped after his companions, who were already scouring away across the prairie.

"The troops behaved most gallantly. They were a handful. They seemed resolved to sell their lives as dearly as they could. Our horses pranced and bounded and could hardly be held as the balls whistled among them. I drew off a little and looked at my husband and my father, both of them still unharmed. I felt that my hour had come, and I tried to forget the people I loved and prepare myself for what was about to happen to me.

"While I was doing that, the surgeon, Dr. Van Voorhees, came up to me. He was badly hurt. His horse had been shot under him and he had taken a ball in the leg. Every muscle in his face was quivering with the agony of terror. He said, 'Do you think they will take our lives? I am badly wounded, but I think not mortally. Maybe we might purchase our lives by promising them a large reward. Do you think there is any chance?'

"'Dr. Van Voorhees,' I said, 'don't let us waste the few moments that yet stay to us in such vain hopes. Our fate is inevitable. In a few moments we must appear before the bar of God. Let us make what preparation is yet in our power.'

"'Oh, I can't die!' he cried. 'I am not fit to die — if I had but a short time to prepare — death is awful!'

"I pointed to Ensign Ronan. He was mortally wounded and nearly down, and he was still fighting with desperation on one knee. 'Look at that man!' I said. 'At least he dies like a soldier.'

"'Yes,' the unfortunate man answered, with a convulsive gasp, 'but he has no terrors of the future — he is an unbeliever!'

"At that moment a young Indian raised his tomahawk at me. I sprang aside and partly avoided the blow, which had been meant for my skull and came down on my shoulder instead. I got both arms around his neck. His scalping-knife hung in a scabbard over his breast and I was doing everything I had to get hold of it when another Indian, an older one, dragged me out of his grasp.
