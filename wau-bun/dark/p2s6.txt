On the afternoon of the 7th of August, Winnemeg — Catfish — a Pottowattamie chief, came in to the post with despatches from General Hull. The despatches said three things. The United States and Great Britain were at war. General Hull had reached Detroit at the head of the Northwestern army. And the island of Mackinac had fallen into the hands of the British.

The orders to Captain Heald were "to evacuate the fort, if practicable, and, in that event, to distribute all the United States' property contained in the fort, and in the United States' factory or agency, among the Indians in the neighborhood."

When he had handed over the despatches, Winnemeg asked to speak with Mr. Kinzie alone. Mr. Kinzie had by then moved his residence inside the fort. Winnemeg told him he knew what the papers said, and asked him to find out whether Captain Heald meant to evacuate the post. He advised strongly against it, and gave his reasons. The garrison was well supplied with ammunition, and had provisions enough to last six months. Far better, he thought, to stay behind the walls until a reinforcement could be sent to their assistance. But if Captain Heald did decide to leave, then he should leave immediately — that day, that hour. The Pottowattamies whose country they would have to cross did not yet know what Winnemeg had been sent to carry. A forced march might get the column through before the ones who wished them harm were ready to stop it.

Captain Heald was told at once what Winnemeg had said, and how earnestly he had said it. He answered that he did intend to evacuate the post. But he had been ordered to distribute the United States' property, and he would not feel justified in leaving until he had gathered in the Indians of the neighborhood and made an equitable division among them.

Winnemeg tried once more, and suggested another expedient. Then march out, he said, and leave every last thing standing where it was. While the Indians were busy dividing the spoils, the troops might get away unmolested. Mr. Kinzie seconded this strongly. It did not meet the approbation of the commanding officer.
