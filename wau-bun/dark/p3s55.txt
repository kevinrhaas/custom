In the course of the spring we had a visit from the Rev. Mr. Kent and Mrs. Kent, of Galena. The occasion is memorable, being the first on which the gospel, according to the Protestant faith, was preached at Fort Winnebago. The large parlor of the hospital was fitted up for the service, and we said it to each other gladly — let us go to the house of the Lord.

For nearly three years we had lived here with no public service of praise and thanksgiving at all. We took this beginning as an omen of better times, and our little sewing-society worked with renewed industry to raise a fund that might one day secure the permanent services of a missionary.

Not long after, on a fine spring morning, while we were seated at breakfast, a party of Indians came into the parlor and up to the door of the room where we were. Two of them went on through and out onto the small portico. The third stayed where he was, standing in the doorway where he had first appeared. He was almost directly opposite me. I raised my eyes, and in spite of the change of dress and the paint he was covered in, I knew him at once.

I went on pouring the coffee. As I poured I said to my husband, "The one behind you, with whom you are speaking, is one of the escaped prisoners."

Mr. Kinzie did not turn his head. He went on listening to all the directions they were giving him about the repairing of their guns and traps and the rest of what they wanted left with the blacksmith. When they had run on a while he turned carelessly toward the parlor door and answered the one who was speaking to him. Then he spoke to me again, and what he said was, "You are right, but it is no affair of ours. We are none of us to look so as to give him notice that we suspect anything. They are undoubtedly innocent, and have suffered enough already."

Contrary to his usual custom, their Father did not ask their names. He wrote out their directions, tied each one to the implement it belonged with, and told them to go and deliver them to M. Morrin themselves.

The rest of our circle were greatly pleased by the young fellow's audacity, and we quite longed to tell the officers that we could have caught one of their fugitives for them, if we had had a mind to.
