One of the soldiers' wives took a resolution that deserves to be recorded. Her name was Mrs. Corbin. From the beginning she had said she would never fall into the hands of the savages, believing that their prisoners were always put to tortures worse than dying.

So when a party came for her, to take her prisoner, she fought them with desperation and would not surrender, though they made signs to her that she would be safe and well treated. She let herself be cut to pieces rather than be taken. That is literally what happened.

There was a Sergeant Holt who took a ball in the neck early in the engagement. Knowing he was badly wounded, he handed his sword up to his wife, who was on horseback near him, and told her to defend herself. Then he made for the lake to get out of the way of the balls. Mrs. Holt rode a very fine horse. The Indians wanted the horse, so they attacked her, meaning to bring her down off it.

They fought her with the butt-ends of their guns only, because they did not want to kill her. She hacked and hewed at the pieces as they were thrust at her, this side, that side, this side again. In the end she broke loose from them and drove out into the prairie. They came after her, shouting and laughing, calling out now and then, "The brave woman! don't hurt her!"

They caught her again eventually, and while she was busy with two or three of them in front, one got behind her and took her by the neck and dragged her off the horse — and she was a large and powerful woman. Their guns were hacked and ruined and several of them were cut badly, and still they seemed to regard her with nothing but admiration. They took her to a trader on the Illinois River, who returned her to her friends. She had received every kindness during her captivity.
