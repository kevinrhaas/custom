With all of that in front of him, their Father judged that nothing short of the utmost watchfulness would do, and that the strongest arguments he had would have to be spent keeping the young men of the Winnebagoes in their allegiance to the Americans. The older men he was sure of. About fifty lodges had come in when the trouble first started and made their camp around our house, and what they told us was that if the Sauks meant to get at us they would have to kill them first. We knew those men. We believed them absolutely.

But having them close, while it made us feel guarded, also opened a channel for the most exciting and agitating communications, and they arrived daily. As the fighting worked nearer and nearer, their runners carried the word in. That Captain Barney's head had been recognized in the Sauk camp, where it had been carried the day before. Then that the Sauks were marching to meet the whites with Lieutenant Beall's head carried on a pole ahead of them. Sometimes a story turned out, unhappily, to be exactly true, as with the murder of their own Agent, M. St Vrain, at Kellogg's Grove — killed by the Sauks themselves, who of all people ought to have protected him.
