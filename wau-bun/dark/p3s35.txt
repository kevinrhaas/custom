We were not left wondering long about the people we had left behind us. Inside of two weeks Old Smoker came again, and he came carrying letters. My husband wrote that General Dodge was with him at Port Winnebago, and that Generals Henry and Alexander were at the Fort as well. The men and the horses were used up — they had been scouring the country after Black Hawk until there was little left in either — and the moment they were recruited enough to move, the whole force would go out again after him, up toward the head-waters of the Rock River. The Winnebagoes had brought in word lately, and by everything that word contained, that was where he would be found.

He told us to put away every uneasiness on his account, and he raised the hope that he would come for us before long, or send. After that our minds sat quieter than they had.

Not many days later I was told, one morning, that a lady was at the front door asking for me. I went down, and the lady was my friend Madame Four-Legs. Her joy at seeing me had its own language: she pressed her two hands together above her forehead and then parted them in a slow waving motion, and laughed, and patted me up and down the arms. Then she reached into her bosom and brought out the letter she had carried to me, which was from my husband, and which said this — "Generals Dodge and Henry left here a few days since, accompanied by Paquette, they met the Sauks near the Wisconsin, on the 21st. A battle ensued, in which upwards of fifty of the enemy were killed - our loss was one killed, and eight wounded. The citizens are well pleased that all this has been accomplished without any aid from Old White Beaver. [56] The war must be near its close, for the militia and regulars together will soon finish the remaining handful of fugitives."
