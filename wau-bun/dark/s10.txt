Sleep took me, but it did not keep me. At the very first hint of dawn something came up to me through the floor, out of the room beneath ours — a chant, low and level, one figure of melody turned over and over, lifting and then sinking again into a kind of grieving. It did not sound to me like anything a living person would make. Wild. Strange. Past all accounting for. I woke my husband in real fear, and he told me, quite calmly, that what I was listening to was the Indians greeting the opening day.

Some Menomonees had been given shelter for the night in the kitchen below us, a kindness offered and a kindness taken; and having kept their custom, which never varies, of singing their morning hymn, they let the sound die away and lay down again to sleep. Their listener overhead did not. I could find nothing in it but beauty — a song of praise raised at daybreak by the untaught sons of the forest. Set that beside the civilized and Christianized world, where so many lie down at night and rise in the morning without one breath of thanksgiving to the Almighty Preserver who keeps them, without so much as a thought of Him who gives His angels charge concerning them. The lesson of that hour has never once faded from me. I have liked to believe that in some of those voices the devotion ran as pure as it runs in the Christian who says the sainted Ken's words, "Awake, my soul! and with the sun," etc.

Callers came to welcome me to the West. Among them were Mr. and Miss Cadle, who stood then at the very first steps of the work that afterward flourished — the education of Indian and half-breed children. Nothing had been built yet. No school-houses, no chapel. We walked over the ground they had chosen for them, and I listened, and was moved by it, while they laid out everything that was to come: the good that would be done, the success that would crown their efforts, the heathen tamed and brought to their Saviour and to the blessings of civilized life. What followed has shown how little the zeal of a few can do when the cupidity of the many stands against it.
