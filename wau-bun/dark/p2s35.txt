Time went on, and each year took a little more out of the hope of getting the little captive back. She, meanwhile, was winding herself closer and closer around the heart of her Indian brother. Nothing could exceed the consideration and affection she was shown, and not by him alone — his mother, the Old Queen, was the same. Every brooch and every string of wampum the lodge owned went onto the child. The principal seat was hers, and the most delicate food was set in front of her first, every time, without discussion. Nothing was left undone that might make her happy, or make her forget the home and the people she came from.

She had watched her parents and her dear little brother go, and felt something close to despair, and for a long time afterward she would not be consoled by anybody. She would rather have died than live apart from everyone she loved. But time does what it always does. It brought its soothing balm, and in the end she was contented, and then happy.

She was quick, and she never stopped moving, and the energy in her was the kind that stayed remarkable in her to the last period of her life. It earned her a name. They called her The Ship under full sail.
