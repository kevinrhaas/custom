It was hard to take in, when we got up the next morning, that twelve miles of prairie were the whole of what lay between us and Chicago le Désiré — which is what I could not stop myself from calling it.

We could see clear across the plain, and at the very edge of it stood two tall trees. My husband told me he had put them in the ground himself, when he was a boy. They had grown high enough by now to serve as landmarks, and they stayed in front of us the whole length of the beaten road. I said the same thing over and over to myself as we went: "There live the friends I am so longing to see! There will end all our trials and hardships!"

A Mr. Wentworth fell in with us on the road, and we asked him for news of the family; it had been a long time since any word of them had reached us. Once we were into Chicago he brought us to a small tavern at the forks of the river. That part of the place was called Wolf Point in those days, after an Indian who had lived there named " Moaway," or "the Wolf."

"Dear me," the old landlady of the little tavern said to us, "what dreadful cold weather you must have had to travel in! Why, two days ago the river was all open here, and now it's frozen hard enough for folks to cross a-horseback!"

My husband did not care to trust it, whatever she said. He decided to leave the horses where they stood and go the rest of the way on foot — about half a mile — to the house where his mother and his sister lived.

So we walked, over the ice first and then along the northern bank of the river. Genevieve caught sight of us — she was a half-breed servant of the family — and she did not stop to greet us. She turned and flew into the house, calling out, - "Oh! Madame Kinzie, who do you think has come? Monsieur John and Madame John, all the way from Fort Winnebago on foot!"

A moment after that we were being held by those dear, kind friends of ours. Somebody was sent off to "the garrison" to bring in the rest of the family, and for that one day, at any rate, I was the wonder and the admiration of the entire circle, "for the dangers I had seen."
